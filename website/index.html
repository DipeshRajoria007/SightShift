    This is a simulation of the Mac app. Camera mode runs a face tracker right in your browser (MediaPipe): video never leaves your device, and the first start downloads about 15 MB.

    01 — How it works

    Look. Pause. Type.

    Your eyes already know where you want to type next. SightShift reads which way your head is turned through the webcam and moves keyboard focus to match, entirely on your Mac.

    STEP 01

    See

    About fifteen times a second, Apple's Vision framework finds your face and measures which way it's turned, plus where your eyes point. Each frame is thrown away right after.

    STEP 02

    Match

    A short calibration, following a dot for about 20 seconds per screen, teaches SightShift where each display sits relative to you. Every click refines it from then on.

    STEP 03

    Type

    Rest your gaze on a screen for 300 ms and the window you last used there has focus, pointer included. On one display, it focuses the window or split pane you look at.

    02 — Features

    Quick to follow, calm by design.

    A focus switcher is only useful if you can forget it's there. SightShift ignores glances, holds still while you work and never fights you for the mouse.

    Every screen, any layout

    Side by side, stacked, or a laptop under a monitor. Turn toward a screen and the window you last used there gets focus. The pointer comes along too, so scrolling lands where you're looking.

    Windows and split panes

    One display is enough. SightShift focuses the window you look at, and the split pane inside terminals and editors.

    typehold

    Holds still while you work

    Focus waits until typing pauses and the mouse rests. Keep typing while you read another screen and it stays where you are.

    No bezel ping-pong

    Going back to a screen needs a slightly bigger turn than going there, so a gaze resting on the gap between monitors can't flip focus back and forth.

    ignored

    Ignores your phone

    Poses aimed away from every screen, at a phone, a notebook or the ceiling, are simply ignored.

    Learns from every click

    You look where you click, so each click teaches SightShift a little more. It keeps up when you sit closer, lean back or shift to one side, and tells you if it ever needs a recalibration.

    ⌃⌥⌘G

    Pauses when you do

    Pause and resume with one shortcut. SightShift also pauses by itself, camera off, whenever your Mac locks or sleeps.

    03 — Works with

    Your terminals, your editors, everything else.

    Window focus works with every Mac app. Split-pane focus works wherever an app exposes its panes to macOS Accessibility.

    Split panes

    • iTerm2
    • Terminal
    • Ghostty
    • cmux
    • Xcode
    • JetBrains IDEs
    • Android Studio

    Focus moves through Accessibility; a terminal that won't accept it gets a click in the middle of the pane.

    Split panes, opt-in

    • VS Code
    • Cursor
    • Windsurf
    • VSCodium
    • Hyper

    Electron apps only reveal their panes when asked, and asking can switch them into screen reader mode, so this one is your call.

    Window focus

    • Any app
    • kitty
    • WezTerm
    • Warp
    • Zed
    • Sublime Text

    Apps that draw their own panes without accessibility information still get window focus. tmux panes need the tmux keys.

    06 — Install

    Set up in about two minutes.

    SightShift builds from source with one command. Then a short setup guide walks you through the permissions and calibration.

    1. Get the codeClone the repository, or download it as a ZIP from GitHub.
    2. Build and installmake install builds the app, signs it locally and copies it to Applications.
    3. Allow the camera and AccessibilityThe setup window explains both and opens the right settings.
    4. CalibrateFollow a dot around each screen, about 20 seconds per screen. Then just look and type.

    # macOS 14+ and Xcode 16+
$ git clone https://github.com/DipeshRajoria007/SightShift.git

$ make install

    07 — Questions

    Good questions.

    Something missing? Open an issue on GitHub.

    Is it eye tracking or head tracking?

    Mostly head tracking. Switching screens relies on which way your head is turned, which an ordinary webcam measures reliably. Eye direction adds finer detail for picking a window or split pane on the same screen.

    Does SightShift record or upload video?

    No. Frames are analysed in memory by Apple's Vision framework and discarded. The app makes no network requests, has no account and collects no analytics.

    Does it work with a single display?

    Yes. Switching screens needs two or more displays, but with one display SightShift focuses the window or split pane you look at.

    Which camera does it use?

    The built-in camera by default. You can pick any other camera in Settings; use one that faces you directly.

    What happens while I'm typing or using the mouse?

    Focus holds still. It moves only once typing has paused (2 seconds by default) and the pointer has rested for 1.5 seconds. If you keep typing straight through a turn, SightShift assumes you're reading the other screen and stays put.

    Does it click on things?

    Focus changes go through macOS Accessibility, so normally nothing is clicked. A few terminals don't let other apps focus a split pane; for those, SightShift clicks the middle of the pane after checking that nothing covers it. You can turn that off, and editors are never clicked.

    How often do I need to calibrate?

    Once per screen, about 20 seconds each, and again only after moving a monitor, the camera or your chair a lot. Your clicks keep refining the model in between.

    Why does it need Accessibility permission?

    To move keyboard focus between windows and panes, and to notice when you're typing or using the mouse so it can hold still.

    Is it really free?

    Yes. SightShift is open source under the MIT license, with no paid tier, trial or account.

    How do I install it?

    Build it from source with one command: clone the repository from GitHub and run make install. You need macOS 14 or later and Xcode 16 or later. The setup guide covers the rest.

    Which Macs are supported?

    macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

    Stop clicking before you type.
